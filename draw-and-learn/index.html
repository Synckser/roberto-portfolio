Draw & Learn: a drawing app kids actually learn from.

A native iOS app where children trace, colour and learn words in their own language, guided by an animated mascot. I designed and built it end to end, from the drawing engine to the App Store release, and I market and maintain it myself.

Why I built it

The problem

Most children's drawing apps are either ad-filled colouring books or blank canvases, and almost all of them are English-only. I wanted a single app where a child can trace a shape, colour it in, and hear the word spoken in their own language, with no ads, no data collection and nothing to get lost in. Nothing on the App Store combined guided tracing, structured vocabulary learning and true multilingual support in one safe place, so I built it myself, end to end, as a solo developer alongside my degree.

Engineering

Under the hood

The parts I'm most proud of, where the interesting engineering lives.

Custom flood-fill engine

Bucket colouring is a hand-written Core Graphics algorithm: an 8-directional ray-cast picks the correct seed pixel even when a child taps on an outline, RGB-sum barriers stop fills at lines, and offline-generated per-guide region maps give pixel-perfect containment with instant response, with no async and no spinners.

MVVM + manager singletons

SwiftUI views back onto focused view models, with 11 ObservableObject managers each owning one concern: sound, speech, localisation, difficulty, progress, subscriptions, replay and more.

Modern concurrency

Strictly async / await, with no Combine. Audio runs on a nonisolated static pipeline off the main thread, while @Published state is mutated on the MainActor to keep the canvas glassy-smooth.

Localisation system

A JSON language-pack loader delivers translations and phonics for 18 languages, with inline bundled fallbacks so the app is never left without content.

StoreKit 2 subscriptions

A transaction listener drives entitlement state, with restore support and a parental gate, built on Apple's current StoreKit 2 APIs. The app is currently released free, and early users are guaranteed to keep everything.

Child-safe by design

No force-unwraps on any path a child can reach, 44pt+ tap targets, graceful fallbacks for missing data, and Reduce Motion support that swaps every animation for a static alternative.

AI asset pipeline

The mascot's voice lines and sound effects are generated with the ElevenLabs and Gemini APIs, driven by Python pipelines that batch-produce, name and normalise audio for every language, turning 505 items × 18 languages of content into a repeatable one-command build instead of thousands of manual recordings.

Problems I hit, and how I solved them

Getting through App Review

ProblemAn early submission was bounced because the in-app purchase products were stuck in "Missing Metadata".

SolutionQueried the App Store Connect API to pinpoint the one missing field per product, then simplified the launch to a free app, resubmitted and got approved.

Proving a kids app is private

ProblemApple's Kids review flagged a data-collection questionnaire on the v1.1 update.

SolutionAudited the compiled app binary: only Apple frameworks, no third-party code, no network or tracking symbols, an empty privacy manifest and every link behind the parental gate. Answered Apple with that evidence and resubmitted.

Release automation

ProblemArchiving, uploading, editing metadata and submitting by hand was slow and easy to get wrong.

SolutionA headless pipeline: xcodebuild archive and export, altool upload, then App Store Connect API calls to attach the build, update localised metadata and submit for review.

Automated store screenshots

ProblemEvery device size and locale needs its own screenshots, and they go stale with each release.

SolutionXCUITest UI tests drive the app into each scene and capture the screenshots, so a new set is one command away.

Bugs only real devices show

ProblemOn my iPhone and iPad, long words like ACCORDION pushed the letter-sound buttons off screen, and Bob started on top of the controls.

SolutionReproduced on device, made the letter row scroll, moved Bob's start position, and re-tested before shipping.

Being found on the store

ProblemOn launch day the generic name did not rank for any search.

SolutionKeyword research, a clearer name and subtitle, listings for the UK, US, Canada and Australia, a custom product page, and capped Apple Search Ads campaigns split into exact keywords and discovery.

Keeping promises to early users

ProblemChanging the price later must not take anything away from families who downloaded early.

SolutionDesigned entitlement around AppTransaction.originalAppVersion, so anyone who installed a free build keeps everything unlocked for good.

Child-safe privacy by design

ProblemKids apps face Apple's strictest rules on data, ads and links.

SolutionEverything runs on-device: no accounts, no analytics, no ads, and an App Privacy label of "Data Not Collected" that I can back up.
