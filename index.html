Real problems I hit, and how I solved them

Getting through App Review

ProblemAn early submission was bounced: in-app purchase products were stuck in "Missing Metadata".

SolutionTraced the exact missing field through the App Store Connect API, simplified the launch to a free app, resubmitted and got approved.

Proving a kids app is private

ProblemApple's Kids review flagged a data questionnaire on the v1.1 update.

SolutionAudited the compiled app: only Apple frameworks, no network or tracking code, every link behind a parental gate. Replied with the evidence and resubmitted.

Slow, error-prone releases

ProblemClicking through archive, upload, metadata and submission by hand wasted hours and caused mistakes.

SolutionBuilt a command-line pipeline: xcodebuild archive and export, upload, then the App Store Connect API to attach the build, update metadata and submit.

Dozens of store screenshots

ProblemEvery device size and English locale needs its own set of screenshots.

SolutionWrote UI tests that drive the app and capture the screenshots automatically, so every release gets fresh, consistent images.

Bugs only real devices show

ProblemOn my own iPhone and iPad, long words (ACCORDION) clipped the letter-sound buttons and Bob covered the top controls.

SolutionReproduced on device, fixed the layout and Bob's starting position, and re-tested before shipping.

Nobody could find the app

ProblemOn launch day the generic name did not rank for any search.

SolutionKeyword research, a clearer name and subtitle, listings for UK, US, Canada and Australia, Apple Search Ads on a strict budget, and short video ads like the ones above.
